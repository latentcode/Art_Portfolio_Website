Margarette






PART I


Five hours had passed and I was still laughing, cackling even. They were unbearably funny no matter how long I stared at them. Resting comfortably within the eye sockets of the creature were the pearlescent eyes of my late wife, Margarette. They were unmistakable, impossibly blue and full of life, shimmering and moist. Now, 20 years after the incident, I saw them again, just as they were, peering at me a few yards from the foot of my bed. What a joyous reunion this was, I couldn’t stop laughing! However, for the life of me, I couldn’t figure out why it was so amusing. Maybe being confined to my chambers had taken its toll on my mind. It was impossible to remember exactly when my nurse stopped visiting but walking to get help was impossible. My legs were rotted to their core, the infection from my untended wound festering deep within my body, crawling up my insides like a tree taking root in the dirt. I was stranded in my bed, left here to stare at them. Those eyes. Those hilarious, godforsaken eyes, dancing in the candlelight.










1815


From across the ballroom, I saw them twinkling, her shiny black heels gliding timidly across the dance floor. It was a cold autumn in Boston, mere months after the war was over, and I locked eyes with the most bewitching woman I had ever seen. She carried herself differently from the other guests. Her wistful aroma cut through the sea of levity surrounding her, as if she wore her misery on her sleeve. Unlike all the poor fools masking their pain with soulless smiles, this woman didn’t try to hide her anguish. She was a black tulip in a garden of lilies; an empty puppet going through the motions, hopelessly trying to blend in with the crowd. Even from far away, I could tell she was a kindred spirit.


“Dancing alone, miss?” I said, walking next to her.


She glanced up at me for a moment before looking down toward the floor. “Yes, I thought I’d try joining the crowd for once,” she remarked quietly, “You soldiers really do make these parties a lot more exciting.”


I put on a smile, “Exciting? That’s not quite the word I’d use.”


“Really now? If this isn’t exciting, I don’t know what is. Everyone’s dancing like it’s their last day on Earth.” She began smiling as well.


“Well, you never know,” I said with a chuckle. I glanced around our hectic surroundings. “You see, I’ve been to plenty of events like this. The orchestra’s playing loud, men and women are dancing arm in arm. It’s all just superficial merriment, my dear. Even after all this mindless frivolity, I have yet to feel, well, anything. But when I saw you moments ago, I felt happier than I’ve been in years. You stand out, I must say.”


Her face turned a slight shade of red. “Oh my! I hope not in a foolish way. I’m not usually out in the open like that. In fact, it was my mother’s idea to dance with the crowd tonight - said I’d been living in my mind for far too long.” She paused briefly, as if she suddenly realized she was sharing too much of her personal life. She collected her thoughts and asked simply, “Goodness, was everyone looking at me?”


“No, you shouldn’t concern yourself with them. You were spectacular.” I replied, “Besides, it’s what’s inside of you that caught my eye. What’s your name, my dear?”


“Margarette,” She said quietly, now looking me directly in my eyes.


Her piercing gaze caught me off guard. I had no idea her eyes were so electrifying, a stark contrast to the aura of dejection she seemed to be saddled with. They were a swirling mixture of blues and greens, an oceanic whirlpool spiralling around her pupils. I felt her gaze peering past my eyes, staring into the back of my skull, reading only the darkest and most repulsive parts of my mind. If that wasn’t enough to scare her off, maybe she truly was the one for me.


“Forgive me if I’m out of place, but I must say, your eyes are the most captivating things I’ve ever seen.”


She darted her eyes away and gave a pained laugh. Her hands began fidgeting, frantically rubbing against each other, almost as if she was trying to keep warm.


“I know. They are extremely frightening, are they not? My father said that when I look at him, my eyes bore deeper into him than a gunshot wound. And he would know, being in the military and all.” She gave a dry chuckle.


She paused briefly and took a deep breath. She quickly glanced at me, studying my expression, then down at the ground again.



“That’s why I’m here, the daughter of the guest of honor, the legendary General Taylor, trying not to ruin his perfect reputation. He doesn’t even want me at these events, you know? But my mother insisted. Now here I am, hopelessly trying to fit in without scaring away the other party-goers.”



“Nonsense,” I quickly chimed in, “Fear was the last thing on my mind when I saw you. If I can be so forward, your gaze fills me with a sense of joy that I can’t even begin to describe. Would you care to dance, Margarette?”



Her expression grew even more pained, like she wanted more than anything to turn me down. She was a person who spent most of her days engulfed by her thoughts; dancing with a stranger like me would be unthinkable. But the unthinkable occurred. From thin air, a resolute expression washed over her face, as if she managed to overcome the natural urge to skulk back into the shadows. She looked up at me once more, her glossy, cosmic eyes resonating with mine.



“It would be my pleasure.”

PART II


My daughter and my daughter’s daughter both inherited them: their color, their shape, their tenderness; it seems the creature crouching in the corner of my room had taken them too. I couldn’t help but feel envious of the being. No matter how malformed its body was, with gangly arms that developed into two paws, a gaunt torso that stretched to the top of my bookshelf, and a spiny and frail neck that supported its enormous head, it was still graced with Margarette’s eyes. Not even covering its face with those long, decaying ears would stop me from getting a closer look.


1817


Swirling around and around and around and around. Those tantalizing, perverted colors. Those irresistible, reprehensible colors. In a spiral.


Twirling her fork around her spaghetti, Margarette sat across the table from me, lost in a trance. She would always spend her evenings like this, spellbound by her thoughts, finding it more pleasurable to relive her memories than to create new ones. To the outside world, she would simply disappear. A sad, empty vessel like me, drawn further and further away from reality by the siren’s call of her woeful musings. However, her long periods of introspection never bothered me. It would give me time to look into her eyes, her strange and intoxicating eyes, looking deeper and deeper in search of their heavenly guidance.


Sometimes, when I stare long enough, the patterns in her eyes would morph into recognizable shapes. Her pupils would shift and transform into the silhouettes of people I know, acting out stories from my past and predictions of the future. Tonight, I saw my father with his rifle traipsing through the forest. I saw my comrades on the battlefield, bloody and wounded. I saw Margarette, crying, shakily lifting a spoon up to her-


Suddenly, a chuckle rang out from across the table, from my wife. It was strange hearing laughter after so long, especially from someone as withdrawn as Margarette. What could someone like her - someone like me - find amusing at a time like this?


“Honey, what are you laughing at?” My words shattered the precious bout of silence. She snapped out of her thoughts, shaken to be ripped so suddenly from her imagination.


“Oh, you startled me!” she smiled nervously. “It was nothing, dear, just a story from my childhood.” She looked over at me, staring intently back at her.


“Tell me about it,” I said coldly. For some reason, I was extremely apprehensive about hearing what she had to say.


She chuckled nervously, then cleared her throat, “Well, alright then, I guess. I have to warn you, it begins rather unpleasantly. You see, the evening before my father left to fight was a tremendously stressful time, especially for him. He… decided to take out his anxiety on me. That night, he screamed and yelled at me for hours, scolding me for being such a recluse, for having these accursed eyes. He called me a ‘stain on the family name.’ In the weeks that followed, I could hardly eat or sleep, replaying his words over and over again in my mind.” Her eyebrows furrowed for a moment. “Anyway, the reason I laughed was that I just remembered what my brother did to cheer me up. This young boy - couldn’t have been more than eight or nine at the time - walked three miles into town and brought me back a stuffed bunny rabbit from the gift shop! I still remember that wonderful feeling when he handed it to me. At that moment, everything felt perfect, like the past had simply washed away. It’s comforting to be able to remember him like that, even though we were so young back then.”


She paused and flashed a fond and subtle smile. “I kept that rabbit, you see. Even after all these years, I still have it. I’m going to give it to Mary once she’s born.” She held her belly lovingly and looked up at me, “Don’t you think that’s a wonderful idea?”


My teeth started to grind at the sentimentality. I ignored her question, “Every single time you tell me about your past, you always have this strange, optimistic view of things, like you can always see a silver lining to every horrible thing that happens to you. If that’s the case - if you’re somehow able to fixate on the good memories over the bad ones - why are you so miserable, then? I… I don’t get it!”


Her smile disappeared. I watched as her face slowly twisted into a grimace, her delicate facial features becoming wrinkly and crude. In our two years of living together, this was the first time I had seen her so irritated. After a few moments, she found her words.


“M-Miserable? Who says I’m miserable? You forget yourself, dear, j-just what are you trying to say?”


My worst fears were coming true. I tried to suppress my emotions, but they burst through nonetheless. Slamming my fist on the table, I shot out of my chair and shouted, “It was unmistakable, Margarette! That night at the ballroom. You couldn’t hide it then and you certainly can’t hide it now. You’re a miserable person, empty and alone, just like me.” I shot her a desperate, pleading smile before exclaiming, “We were made for each other!”


My exasperated shouting left me in a coughing fit. It took some time to catch my breath as my words hung viciously in the air.


Margarette replied hesitantly, “… Dear, I don’t know what you think you saw in me, but it simply is not the case.” She chose her words carefully so as not to set me off again. “I may not be very social - for my father’s sake I wish I was - but just because I keep to myself doesn’t mean I’m unhappy.”


“Please, just stop pretending, dear,” I beseeched her. The more she spoke, the further I sank.


“Look John, I’m far from perfect. I cling to my memories because… because each second I spend in the real world is utterly draining. The responsibility - the independence - it’s all so suffocating! I’m no longer the child I wish I was. My life rests entirely in my hands now and I just don’t know what to do with it. Every day I pray my brother will come over with another silly present and assure me that everything is fine. I pray he’ll tell me what to do and what to say and how to act. I’ve come to realize, moments like that only happen in my mind. I… I’m happy in my mind, John.”


Her hands instinctively began rubbing together, as they always seem to do in times of distress. She sighed, “Sometimes, it is easier to live in the past. Is that so wrong?”


I glared at her. The stranger across the table, eating my food, carrying my child, pretending to be my wife.


“I can’t believe I thought you understood me,” I said harshly. She let out a sharp gasp. I watched her eyes widen in disbelief. “It must be amazing to duck out of reality as you do,” I continued, “to be able to slip into peace at a moment's notice. God, I envy you! It’s a damn shame I don’t have any warm childhood memories to fall back on. I have to endure each second of each minute of every day, hollow and alone, with nothing but my horrid thoughts to keep me company. How naive was I, to think I found someone who truly shared my pain?!”


She stared back, appalled at my words. “I-I-I,” she stammered, “I had no idea you felt that way. I really didn’t, John, I-I don’t even know what to say.”


My head was pounding, my throat was on fire. Pinching the bridge of my nose, I shifted my gaze toward the floor. Everything went blurry.


“Nobody knows me. Nobody knows me,” I muttered hoarsely under my breath. She was my buoy, the only thing keeping me afloat, keeping me from sinking into the unknowable depths of my mind. My one and only shred of companionship. Now, it was just me and the endless ocean. The endless expanse of blue and green, swirling around and around and around and around, in a spiral.


“Wait,” I whispered. A smile crept onto my face as I began making sense of my situation. With one thought, the whole world came back into focus. Looking up at her, I said, slowly and clearly, “It was never you who I fell in love with, Margarette. It was your eyes.”


They began to water. Before long, her pearlescent eyes erupted with rivers of tears. I heard sniffles waft through the air from across the table.


A voice spoke out, choking back sobs, "Please stop looking at me like that, John. Your horrible expression… it's like the last time my father looked me in the eyes… that look of utter depravity! I can't stand it! Why… why do you keep staring at me like that?!"


“Shhh, your eyes are telling a story.”

PART III


Wiping the tears from my face, I took a deep breath as I attempted to stifle my laughter. I had to see them up close again, to gaze into the boundless sea, to have them envelop my vision such that even my periphery shone blue. I steeled myself before crawling out of bed and dropping to the hardwood below. The crash of my body hitting the floor made the creature wince and back further into the unlit corner of my bedroom. Only her eyes were visible anymore, their glow reminiscent of the beam of light emitted by a lighthouse, guiding me toward salvation.

1817 - PRESENT DAY


Wherever I looked, I saw darkness. It felt as if time had stopped. I cannot believe she would do that! To deprive me of what’s mine?!


For years, she would try to hide them from me. She would avoid looking at me, trying to duck past my line of sight. But our apartment wasn’t that big; I would always find a vantage point. Soon, she began covering them with a blindfold. She learned how to do her daily duties around our home without being able to see. She would care for Mary, trying to keep her out of the house as long as possible so I couldn’t peer into her eyes either. As soon as Mary turned of age, she forced her to move out. Claimed it was to protect her from me, to rid our household of another pair of accursed eyes. It didn’t matter. As long as I had my wife with me, I had her eyes as well.


She never made it easy for me. She knew I was desperate to take a peek - to finally catch a glimpse of the end of their tale. But no matter the pleading and begging and negotiating, she refused to let me win. She was too weak to leave, to live life on her own, but when it came to disobeying me? She was exceptional. Top of the class, best in the business! I mean really, how selfish could one person be?


Every day, I saw her slipping more and more. I saw her taking off her blindfolds when she thought I wasn’t around, finding someplace where she could sneak off and read her novels. In the end, she would always catch me staring. She would scream at me, “I could have lived if it wasn’t for you!” It was ludicrous. She would be too busy daydreaming to function in the real world. Even now, hiding in her mind couldn’t save her; being lost in her thoughts only left her more vulnerable. “You don’t deserve my eyes,” she would growl whenever I caught sight of them, evidence that her Southern politeness had since corroded away into pure venom. Her words were bitter and vindictive. Until she stopped talking altogether.


One day, I woke up and sat at the kitchen table. She was making my daily cup of tea as usual before I headed off to work. She no longer seemed angry at me, at least not outwardly. Her presence felt completely resigned. That morning she carried herself differently, weaker than usual. Her shoulders were slouched as if she had suddenly given up on correcting her posture. Her legs were unstable, at risk of buckling in on themselves. Watching her from the back, it seemed she had not showered yet; her hair was frizzy, and there was an awful odor emanating from her.


“Dear, have you forgotten to bathe?” I asked plainly. A faint, guttural groan slipped out of her mouth.


She carried through the motions of making tea. Her hands shook as she removed the tea bag from the water. When she stirred, it made such a dreadful racket that I was worried the cup would shatter. She finally placed the beverage on a tray, then turned around to set it on my table.

One after another, a dark substance dripped into the mug from above. The splashing caused the tea to ripple and redden before my eyes. In a wild panic, I glanced up to see what she had done. A debilitating sense of dread overwhelmed me as I saw her. Covering the top half of her face was a filthy bandage, soaked through with what remained of her savage butchery: the putrid remnants of her precious eyes. Her face was empty and brutalized. Her eye sockets, hollow. Behind her bloodied nightgown was the vacant vessel of a former human.


“What have you done?! You monster! You witch!” I fell to the floor in a terrible coughing fit. Fire spread through me, erupting from the pit of my stomach and spreading to the very tips of my fingers. I looked up at her.


After 18 years of torment, I saw her smile again. A bloody, crooked smile. That’s when my vision turned black.


My surroundings were shrouded in darkness. By carving out her own, she rendered my own eyes blind. I could feel myself moving subconsciously in the shadows, but I could not see. My purpose was stolen from me. For the first time in years, I was empty again. My body was a puppet, acting out whatever thoughts still lingered in the back of my mind.


Maybe half a day had passed when I finally saw the light of day again. Those hours of darkness felt like an eternity. I was out in the woods now, where the wind whistled and the harsh winter chill bit through my jacket. The sun warmed my back as it began to set. I was covered in dirt. And there they were, dangling from a branch by their optic nerves: Margarette’s eyes. They illuminated the surrounding area, softly swaying back and forth over a loose patch of soil. My soul felt alive again. I fell to my knees and began laughing. I laughed so hard I began sobbing. It was like gazing upon God. Rejuvenated, I picked up my shovel and headed back home, whistling a jolly tune on the way.


I was fifty when I last saw Margarette. As for her eyes, I saw them everywhere after that. Floating in my morning tea, strung high above me from the rafters, pinned to my bedroom door. The stories they would tell, extravagant and wicked. My dreams laid plain before me. I became the head of my job, usurping that horrible man who ruled over me for years. I found my father, that loathsome bastard, and reclaimed all the years he stole from me. I met someone beautiful, as depraved and beastly as I was. I felt… whole. In every vision I had, there was a figure watching over me. Guiding me. An enormous bunny rabbit, twisted and deformed, with a terribly long neck and a rotted skeletal body. My guardian angel, telling me what to do and what to say and how to act. I owe it all to him.


It was the last stretch of my journey, and I was eager to see how it would end. I was walking outside when I saw the door to my house glowing. Staring through the peephole, I saw one of her eyes staring back from the other side. Gradually, her pupils transformed into a scene. It showed me lying on the ground. I was looking up toward something, presumably something incredible. I reached my hand out toward it, inching ever closer, touch-


Crack!


The first strike hit me in the head. As I fell to the floor, I saw a crowd of angry men and women standing over me. “We know what you did!” shouted one. “You savage! Your poor wife!” shouted another. They didn’t let up. Reality struck me over and over again. The police may not have been able to link me to a crime, but they knew. Everybody knew. The mob tore into me with makeshift weapons, unbridled fury behind every swing. My legs took the brunt of most of it, becoming bloody and mangled. Every time they hit my body, my vision faded more and more. By the time the sheriff came, I was blind.


I spent the next twenty years in my bed. With the money I had left, I hired a nurse to tend to me, but she couldn’t save my legs. Every day, more and more of my strength left my body as I was spoon-fed by a stranger. At least I couldn’t see any of the humiliation. Margarette’s eyes did not appear again after the beating, and with their absence, only darkness remained. Month after month passed like this, wasting away until the nurse stopped coming, and I was left to rot. Unable to stand. Unable to see. Unable to cope.


Then, just as I lost hope, God answered my prayers. A familiar light cut through the darkness. Those delicious blues and greens came back to haunt me once again. Swirling around and around and around and around, in a spiral. I couldn’t stop laughing!


I had found my purpose once again.




PART IV


I crawled toward them, gripping the floorboards as I pulled myself forward, my withered legs dragging behind me. Further into the darkness I went. My excitement was feral, bubbling into more desperate fits of laughter as I eagerly wormed my way over to them. As my vision adjusted to the dark, I realized I was now looking up at the creature, its neck stretching a good seven feet above me. Finally, they looked down to see me; I locked eyes with Margarette once more, with the same expression she had when we first spotted each other across the ballroom. Propping myself up, I reached my arm toward the creature. Like a wild animal investigating a strange smell, it meekly craned its neck down to observe me more closely. Her eyes were inches away from mine, bloodshot and beautiful. Before I knew it, I had extended my finger, making contact with its smooth and wet surface, feeling the ocean blue consume me, the divine pearlescence invading my brain and worming its way under my skin, filling me with unspeakable delight before I collapsed dead on the floor.
